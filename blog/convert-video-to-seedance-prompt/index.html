Convert a Video to a Seedance Prompt, Step by Step

Published October 13, 2026 · 7 min read

The grammar is one thing; the production line is another. This is the repeatable six-step path from a clip you admire to a Seedance-shaped prompt you can paste and render — with the two decision points where most people go wrong marked explicitly.

  2. Steps 1-2: source and extraction

The six-step conversion

1 excerpt → 2 extract → 3 reformat into clauses → 4 repair the camera line → 5 declare constants → 6 test render one variable at a time. Steps 1 and 4 are where quality is won; the rest is transcription. Budget five minutes for the first clip and two for each one after.

Steps 1–2: source and extraction

Excerpt one continuous shot of 6–12 seconds — no cut inside it, no title card, no overlay text. If the reference has four shots, you have four conversions, not one. This matters more with this model family than any other, because Seedance handles a single movement cleanly and averages two of them into a drift.

Extract. Paste the URL into SocialToPrompt — 27 named platforms, plus MP4/MOV/WebM upload, which skips the 1-credit extraction step and goes straight to prompt generation (3 credits; a full link run on a clip up to 20 seconds is 4). Take the fields it returns after the stated 20–40 seconds: subject, camera, lighting, pacing, style.

Steps 3–4: reformat and repair

Reformat into the clause order — subject and framing, one camera verb, then what changes and the end state — moving light, palette and material to short trailing clauses. Full grammar: Seedance prompt structure.

Repair the camera line by hand. This is decision point two. Scrub two seconds of the source and answer three questions: do the frame corners sweep (rotation → pan/tilt), does the background grow without parallax (zoom) or with parallax (dolly/truck), and does the horizon move vertically (crane)? Then write the verb you observed, not the one the extractor guessed. Stabilised gimbal work is the most commonly mis-reported thing in short-form footage.

Step 5: constants and aspect

Anything you want identical across a sequence must be restated in every shot prompt: same subject noun, same light source and direction, same palette words, same aspect ratio, same format claim (35mm feel, phone video, macro). Then state duration and the end frame. Multi-shot coherence is mostly this step, and it is the difference between a sequence and a pile of similar-looking clips.

One vertical-specific note: with 9:16 output, “medium shot” frames differently than in landscape, so add the crop behaviour you want — “subject fills frame height, tight headroom”.

Step 6: test render and iterate

Reference clip: hand pours espresso, café window light, 8s, one shot.

Extracted:      barista pours espresso into a cup, warm light, close-up, smooth camera,
                cinematic coffee shop atmosphere, shallow depth of field.
Seedance-ready: macro close-up, espresso pouring into a ceramic cup on a steel bar,
                camera locked-off at counter height, slight handheld drift;
                soft window key from camera-left, warm highlights on the crema;
                steam rises and dissipates; cup fills and pour stops, ends on the full cup;
                shallow depth of field, muted warm grade, 6s, 9:16.

Note the four upgrades: “smooth camera” became an honest locked-off frame with micro-drift, “warm light” acquired a direction, the shot acquired an ending, and the pour acquired a physical state change. Render that once, compare at the same size, then change one clause per attempt — the discipline is in the eight-slot template.

If you are converting short-form rather than a raw clip, the platform-specific traps live in TikTok to Seedance and Instagram Reels extraction; for other model families see Veo and Kling.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert a video into a Seedance prompt?

Excerpt one continuous shot, extract its structured fields with a video-to-prompt tool, then reformat as subject-framing, one camera verb, and what changes by the end. Add light direction, palette, duration and aspect ratio, and repair the camera verb yourself against the source.

Which step do people skip and regret?

Verifying the camera verb. Extractors routinely report gimbal glides as static shots and tripod frames as slow push-ins, and with this model family a wrong movement word is the single biggest cause of a render that looks nothing like the reference.

Should I convert the whole video or one shot?

One shot. A multi-scene clip produces an averaged description that fits none of its shots, and the video models handle a single movement cleanly while averaging two into drift. Convert four shots as four prompts, then cut them together.

How do I keep a Seedance sequence consistent?

Restate the constants in every shot prompt: identical subject noun, same light source and direction, same palette words, same format and aspect ratio. Models have no memory between generations, so wording drift becomes footage drift.
