How to Extract a Prompt From a Video Online, Without Setting Anything Up

Published October 13, 2026 · 7 min read

Everything a clip’s prompt needs is already in the clip: the shot size, the camera move, the light direction, the cut rhythm, the grade. Extracting it is a reading job, not a writing job — and doing it online means you skip the part that stops most people, which is setting up a local pipeline of frame grabbers and vision models. This is the browser-only route end to end, including the input formats that work, what the analysis does while you wait, and the checks worth running before you spend a credit on the wrong video.

The tool we use for it is SocialToPrompt, which pairs a multi-platform downloader with a prompt engine; the credit economics are covered separately in our free tier breakdown, so this page is about the workflow.

What “online” changes about the job

Three things, and only one of them is convenience:

  • No environment. The DIY version of this is yt-dlp to fetch the clip, ffmpeg to sample frames, and a vision model API you pay for and wire up. Online, that whole stack is one page.
  • Temporal analysis, not frame guesses. A frame sampler sees five stills and infers motion. A service that ingests the clip can look at change across time, which is where camera movement and pacing live — the fields people most often get wrong by hand.
  • Your link leaves your machine. That is the trade. Anything you paste is visible to the service, so clips that are not yours to share — unreleased product footage, a client’s NDA shoot — belong in the local route, not a browser tab.

Two inputs: a link or a file

The link path is the fast one, and the supported surface is wide. The homepage names 27 platforms — YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, X, Vimeo, Twitch, Reddit, Bilibili, Kuaishou, Xiaohongshu, Pinterest, Snapchat, LinkedIn, Dailymotion, Rumble, Kick, SoundCloud, Mixcloud, Bandcamp, Twitter, Tumblr, 9GAG, VK, Niconico, PeerTube, Odysee — and adds that anything yt-dlp can reach works too, which in practice means most of the video web.

The upload path matters more than people expect. If the clip is already on your drive, uploading an MP4, MOV or WebM skips the extraction step entirely, which is both faster and cheaper: the site prices extraction at 1 credit and prompt generation at 3, so a file you already hold can go straight to the expensive half. Use it when the source is a private export, a screen recording, a downloaded ad you keep for reference, or a platform link that refuses to resolve.

Link hygiene saves wasted runs: use the canonical URL for the single video (not a playlist, channel or search page), and strip campaign parameters from links copied out of email or Slack.

The browser pipeline, minute by minute

Press Extract prompt and the page narrates the work in seven visible stages: extracting frames, reading video content, understanding motion, analysing camera and movement, analysing style, detecting visual aesthetics, then generating the prompt. The site states this usually takes 20–40 seconds, and in ordinary use that is the whole wait — there is no queue, no email callback and no job dashboard to come back to.

Two practical notes from running it. First, the stage list is a useful diagnostic: if a run feels slow, the motion and camera stages are where time goes, because they need the most frames. Second, do not navigate away mid-run — the prompt is produced in that session, and losing it costs a credit like any other failure.

Output: prompt, .txt and language

You get a structured prompt, not a paragraph, with three ways to keep it: Copy prompt, Download .txt, or Download video for the source clip. The .txt is the one that compounds — a folder of timestamped prompt files becomes a reference library, while clipboard prompts evaporate.

Before you run, set the output language. The interface offers roughly 20 languages, so a prompt can come back in the language you actually write generation prompts in. If you work in Chinese, Spanish or Arabic, that removes a translation pass that would otherwise quietly corrupt the camera vocabulary — “dolly in” survives machine translation about as well as you would hope.

Pre-flight checks before you spend a credit

  1. Is it short? The stated 4-credit full run covers videos up to 20 seconds. Feed it a 20-second excerpt of the shot you care about; feed a 4-minute video to a per-credit tool and you are paying for summary rather than detail.
  2. Is it public? Private, age-gated or login-only posts will not resolve. Copy the link from a logged-out window if you are unsure.
  3. Is the shot you want actually in the first 20 seconds? If not, upload the trimmed segment instead of the full video.
  4. Does it have motion worth extracting? A static talking-head gives you a subject, a setting and a grade. That is fine — just know you are paying for three fields, not six.

And one honest counter-check: if what you want is a written summary of what is said in a video, you do not need a prompt engine at all — grab the transcript instead, which our guide to downloading YouTube captions covers in three formats.

Where online beats a local script

A local pipeline wins on privacy, cost at volume and reproducibility. An online tool wins on the other 95% of cases: no setup, motion-aware analysis rather than still-frame guessing, and prompt-shaped output you can paste into a generator immediately. If you extract prompts weekly, the browser route is the right default and the script is a project for when volume makes it worth maintaining. Frequently asked questions

Can I extract a prompt from a video without installing anything?

Yes. The whole workflow runs in a browser tab: paste a supported platform link or upload a video file, wait through the analysis stages, then copy the prompt or download it as .txt. Nothing is installed locally and there is no API key to configure.

Which video sources work for online prompt extraction?

SocialToPrompt names 27 platforms on its homepage — including YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, X, Reddit, Pinterest, Bilibili and Kuaishou — and says it supports anything yt-dlp can reach, plus direct upload of MP4, MOV and WebM files. Private, age-gated and login-only links are the exception.

How long does online prompt extraction take?

The tool states 20 to 40 seconds for a typical run, and the interface narrates the stages as they happen: frames, content, motion, camera, style, aesthetics, then the prompt itself. Longer or more complex clips lean toward the top of that range.

Why should I upload a file instead of pasting a link?

Three reasons: the clip is not published anywhere, the platform link will not resolve, or you want to skip the extraction step. Extraction is billed separately from prompt generation, so a file you already own can go straight to the prompt half.
