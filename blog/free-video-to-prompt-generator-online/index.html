Free Video to Prompt Generator Online: Four Routes Compared

Published October 13, 2026 · 9 min read

There are four real ways to get a prompt out of a video without paying, and each is free in a different currency: one costs credits, one costs setup time, one costs a subscription you may already have, and one costs accuracy. Comparing them on price alone guarantees a bad decision, so this is the comparison on what each route actually leaves you with.

What counts as free here

Four definitions are circulating, and they are not equivalent:

  • Free to try — an account-gated service with a small allowance. No money, but limited output and usually a time window.
  • Free to use, unlimited — rare in this niche because video analysis has real compute costs.
  • Free with your own compute — open-source tools you run locally; the software costs nothing and your time and hardware do.
  • Free because bundled — a capability inside a subscription you already pay for.

Below, each route in order of how little setup it needs.

Route 1: hosted extractor with a free tier

SocialToPrompt is the representative of this class, and its terms are published: every new account gets 3 credits valid for 24 hours, no card required, and a full run costs 4 credits — 1 to extract the video, 3 to generate the prompt — on clips up to 20 seconds. So the free allowance buys three downloads or one prompt without extraction, but not a complete link-to-prompt cycle.

  • Free in: zero setup, motion and camera analysed over time, structured prompt output, 27 platforms plus MP4/MOV/WebM upload, ~20 output languages.
  • Free in quotes: the trial is one day and non-cumulative; deeper use is $2.90 for 50 credits, $5.90 for 150, $12.90 for 400, each pack valid 30 days.
  • Not for: confidential footage, or people who will not create an account.

The credit arithmetic is worked through in what the free tier buys.

Route 2: DIY frames and a vision model

The genuinely no-cost-to-run path: pull frames locally, describe them to any vision model, and impose the slot structure yourself.

yt-dlp -f "bv*[height<=480]+ba/b" --output ref.mp4 "URL"      # where you're entitled to
ffmpeg -i ref.mp4 -vf fps=1 frames/f_%03d.png                  # one frame per second
# then pass a few frames to a vision model with this instruction:
# "Write a video generation prompt with fields: subject, shot size, camera movement,
#  lighting, pacing, style. One sentence per field. Do not invent movement."

Costs nothing beyond a model you already use; scales to any volume; nothing leaves your machine. The honest weakness is motion: a set of stills cannot distinguish a locked-off camera from a slow push, and cannot see a half-second whip pan at all — which is the whole subject of how motion analysis works. Expect to write the camera line yourself, plus 10–15 minutes per clip.

Route 3: chat tools with video or frame input

If you already pay for a multimodal assistant, you may already own 80% of this. Attach the clip, or a contact sheet of frames, and ask for the same field-structured output — several accept video files directly, subject to length and size limits, and to the terms of the plan you are on.

What you gain: nothing to buy, and good prose output. What you lose: structure (it will give you paragraphs unless you demand fields), no download of the source clip, no batch tooling, and the same motion blind spot as route 2 when the model is really working from frames. Best for one-offs where you want a description of a video you already have.

Route 4: captions as a shortcut

For a specific and large category — people who want a prompt because they want the content of a video, not its look — captions are the free answer. On YouTube the spoken layer is already text, downloadable as TXT, SRT or VTT without an account, and a transcript often carries more of what made the video work than any shot description would. Our caption downloader review and text extractor guide cover the mechanics; the transcript workflow covers the long-video case.

What it will not give you is anything visual: no camera, no lighting, no grade.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a genuinely free video to prompt generator?

There are four kinds of free. Hosted tools give a trial allowance — SocialToPrompt's is 3 credits for 24 hours, while a full run costs 4. A DIY pipeline with yt-dlp, ffmpeg and a vision model costs nothing to run but takes setup and can't measure motion well. Multimodal chat tools are free if you already pay for them. And for content rather than visuals, captions are free outright.

Which free route gives the best camera descriptions?

The hosted route, because it analyses the clip over time rather than from sampled stills. Frame-based methods cannot distinguish a locked-off camera from a slow push-in, and miss sub-second moves entirely — which is the field that matters most in a reusable prompt.

Can I do this without creating an account?

Yes, via the local route: extract the clip, sample frames, and structure the description yourself with a vision model. It costs ten to fifteen minutes per clip and your own judgement on movement, but nothing leaves your machine — the only acceptable option for confidential footage.

Do free tiers work on long videos?

Poorly, and not because of pricing. A single prompt can't describe a multi-scene video, and analysis quality drops as the window widens. Excerpt one shot of 6 to 12 seconds regardless of which route you use.
