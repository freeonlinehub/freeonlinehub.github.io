FreeOnline.fyi Review: 69 Free Tools in Your Browser

Published September 13, 2026 · 9 min read

We spend a lot of time cataloguing free tools from across the web, so when a site shows up that builds its own tools instead of listing other people’s, we pay attention. FreeOnline.fyi is exactly that: a self-contained collection of 69 small, single-purpose tools that all run in your browser — no account, no install, no paywall. We clicked through all five categories over a few days. Here’s the honest verdict.

What is FreeOnline.fyi?

FreeOnline.fyi describes itself as a “growing collection of 69 free online tools to make your life easier,” under the tagline “Useful Tools. A Simpler Internet.” Unlike directories that link out to other websites, every tool here is built into the site itself: open a card, and the tool runs right there in the browser. The pitch is three-fold — no signup, simple & fast, built for everyone — and in our testing the site actually delivers on all three.

The collection is organised into five filterable groups: calculators, converters, generators, formatters and a catch-all “more tools” section, with a search bar up top and two pages of listings.

What’s inside: the five categories

Calculators (10 tools)

The largest and, honestly, the most interesting category. Alongside the universal staples — a percentage calculator, a college GPA calculator, a BMR and calorie calculator — you’ll find a 401(k) projector for US savers, an India GST calculator, a Saudi building-cost calculator, an Alinma mortgage calculator and a Morocco end-of-service-benefits calculator. That mix of generic and fiercely local is rare.

Generators (7 tools)

Practical, commerce-flavoured utilities: a receipt maker, QR-code and barcode generators, a video thumbnail maker, a background remover, a product mockup generator — and a delightfully specific French word-search maker for teachers.

Converters (6 tools)

The essentials, done simply: video conversion, a PDF-to-Word text cleaner, WebP-to-JPG conversion, a YouTube-to-MP3 planner and a USD-to-SAR currency converter.

Formatters (2 tools)

Thin but useful for developers: a JSON formatter/viewer and an online HTML/CSS/JS editor. Fine for quick fixes; for heavier work you’ll still want the dedicated tools in our free developer tools category.

More tools (15 tools)

The long tail, and honestly part of the charm: a German AI text generator, a pregnancy conception-date calculator, a tarot card reading, a fuel-cost calculator and more. It’s the kind of shelf where you arrive for one thing and leave with three.

The standout: region-specific calculators

Most free tool sites serve the default market: English, US-centric, done. FreeOnline.fyi goes visibly further. Tools carry explicit language and locale tags — EN, FR, DE, AR, with region markers for the US, UK, India, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Morocco and France — and the calculators are genuinely local, not just translated. An India GST calculator, a Morocco end-of-service calculator and an Alinma mortgage calculator solve problems that generic “loan calculators” simply don’t.

For anyone building or serving audiences outside the US, this is the site’s most valuable idea, and we’d love to see other directories steal it.

Privacy and the no-signup approach

The second thing we appreciated: most tools state plainly that nothing is uploaded or stored — the processing happens client-side in your browser. That matters for the converter and generator tools, where the usual worry is what happens to the file you just dragged into a web page. No accounts also means no email harvesting, no upsell emails, no retention dark patterns. The site even carries a disclaimer that calculator results are estimates for reference only — a refreshingly honest touch for a free tools site.

Where it falls short

No honest review is a victory lap. Three things kept FreeOnline.fyi from a flawless score:

  • No editorial layer. Tools are listed with name and one-liner, but there are no ratings, reviews or “best for” guidance — you pick blind and find out by trying. Pagination splits the catalogue across two pages where richer filtering would serve better.
  • Depth over breadth per tool. These are deliberately small, single-purpose utilities. Need batch video conversion or a full PDF workflow? A dedicated tool beats a mini-tool every time — see our picks for the best free PDF tools as an example.
  • Discoverability. With 69 tools and light metadata, the search box does a lot of work. Misspell it and you’re prompted to try a different word.

Who it’s for

FreeOnline.fyi is at its best for quick, one-off tasks: converting a file once, checking one number once, generating one QR code. It’s also genuinely strong for non-US audiences — if you need an India GST figure or a Gulf-region mortgage estimate, this is one of the few free sites that takes you seriously. Power users with repeat workflows will still want dedicated tools; casual users will love the zero-commitment model.

Verdict

4 out of 5. FreeOnline.fyi does one thing — small, private, instant browser tools — remarkably well, and its regional calculator coverage is the best we’ve seen on a free site. It won’t replace a proper toolbox for heavy work, and it doesn’t try to. Bookmark it for the moments between the moments.

Frequently asked questions

Is FreeOnline.fyi really free?

Yes. Every tool we tried ran without a paywall, account or trial limit. The site states it is 100% free with no hidden fees, and nothing we clicked asked for sign-up or payment.

Do I need to create an account to use it?

No. All 69 tools run in the browser with no signup, which the site lists as a core value proposition. You open a tool, use it, and leave.

Are my files and data uploaded to a server?

Mostly no. The site emphasises that its tools run client-side in your browser and that nothing is uploaded or stored. That said, the site itself tells users its calculators are estimates for reference only — treat sensitive or high-stakes numbers accordingly.

How does FreeOnline.fyi compare to FreeToolsHub?

They take different approaches. FreeOnline.fyi builds its own 69 single-purpose browser tools with strong regional coverage, while FreeToolsHub is a hand-picked directory of the best free tools from across the web, organised by category and reviewed by editors. They complement each other rather than compete.
