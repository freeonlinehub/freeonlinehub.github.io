Gen3dly Review: Text & Image to 3D Models (2026)

Published November 15, 2026 · 9 min read

You need a 3D model. Maybe a 360° spin for a product listing, a prototype you can rotate in a meeting, a prop for a game greybox, or a desk toy for the printer sitting idle in the corner. The traditional answer is three months of Blender tutorials or a freelancer quote. Gen3dly offers a third answer: describe the object in plain language — or upload a single photo — and get back a textured model you can preview, rotate, and export.

We walked the full path the site documents, from signup to export, and checked every claim that matters — free-tier size, per-model cost, export formats, commercial rights, and whether the meshes survive contact with a real printer — against Gen3dly’s own pricing page, its free-tier walkthrough, and its about page. What Gen3dly actually does

Gen3dly is an AI 3D creation platform with three generation inputs and one pipeline. Text to 3D turns a written prompt into a model. Image to 3D reconstructs a model from a single reference photo. AI texturing generates realistic materials and finishes on top of the geometry. Every generation lands in a live 3D preview — drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, switch finishes — so you inspect the asset before you spend anything on exporting it.

The workflow is three steps and contains no modelling software: describe your idea (or upload an image), let the platform generate the asset, preview and download. Exports cover GLB, GLTF, OBJ, and STL, with FBX listed as planned — confirm it is actually offered in the app before building a pipeline around it, since the site notes that only supported formats appear at export time. The public gallery shows the range it aims at: characters, animals, game assets, architecture pieces, and toys, each tagged with the input mode that produced it.

Your first model in about 15 minutes

The fastest meaningful test follows the plan in Gen3dly’s own free-tier walkthrough:

  1. Sign up. Account creation is live on the site; the free tier needs no card.
  2. Pick your input. Text when nothing exists yet; a clean photo — single subject, uncluttered background — when you have one.
  3. Write a concrete prompt. Their walkthrough’s example is the right shape: chubby gray cat sitting, closed happy eyes, smooth matte finish, cute desk figurine style, 3d printable. Subject, pose, finish, style, intended use — in that order.
  4. Inspect like a skeptic. Rotate the preview fully and check the underside. On the free tier a regeneration costs another 20 credits you do not have, so the inspection pass is not optional.
  5. Export for the job. GLB for web and engines, STL for the slicer, OBJ for design tools — more on this below.

Diagnostic pass: if the silhouette reads correctly from every angle and the texture sits where it should, the pipeline works end-to-end for your use case. If the back side collapses into mush, the problem is usually the input — an occluded subject or a one-sided photo — rather than the wording. Re-shoot or re-prompt before assuming the tool failed.

What the free tier actually buys

This is the part most AI 3D generators obscure, so credit where it is due: Gen3dly states it plainly. New accounts get 20 welcome credits, no card required, and one finished model costs exactly 20 credits. Every export format is included. The free tier is therefore one complete model, end to end — a genuine trial of the whole pipeline, not a watermarked demo.

That single-model budget shapes strategy. Do not spend it learning the interface; spend it on the asset you actually want, with the inspection discipline from the previous section. One careful generation that you export and use beats three curious clicks that teach you nothing. If one model answers your question — does this pipeline produce something my printer / engine / listing can use? — the free tier did its job.

Pricing, and the per-model math

Beyond free, the structure is credit packs and subscriptions, with one refreshingly simple rule: every completed model costs 20 credits regardless of quality, material, or export format. No surge pricing for high-quality, no format upcharge.

The per-model math is nearly flat: about $1.00 on Starter, $1.00 on Plus, and roughly $0.86 on Pro. So the tiers are not a volume discount story — they are a throughput story. Pay for Plus or Pro when generation becomes the bottleneck in your workflow (models waiting on you is fine; you waiting on models is the upgrade trigger), for the priority queue, and for Pro’s higher-quality output. If your printer sits idle waiting for models, upgrade. If models sit unprinted, don’t. On commercial rights: usage follows the licence of the plan you are on plus the underlying model providers’ terms, so check the licence shown at export time before an asset goes into a product you sell.

Where the models actually go

Format choice is the difference between a model you admire and a model you use. The mapping:

  • GLB / GLTF keep materials and are the right pick for real-time engines, the web, and game greyboxes — Gen3dly’s own game-dev guide imports GLB straight into Unity.
  • OBJ is the broadly compatible choice for design and DCC tools.
  • STL goes to the slicer — with one mandatory stop first (below).

The 3D-printing caveat, stated plainly: AI-generated meshes can contain thin walls, non-manifold geometry, or floating parts. Gen3dly’s own FAQ says generated models are not automatically print-ready. Treat every export as a starting point: run a repair pass and a slicing check in your printing software before committing filament. The honest framing is generate-then-finish, and the finishing toolchain (slicers, repair tools, model libraries) is free — the generation is the part you budget credits for.

Who it is for, and who should skip it

Reach for it when: you sell products and want 3D spins or rapid prototypes without a modelling hire; you are an indie dev blocking out characters, props, and set pieces before a production pass; you are a maker who wants printable starting points in minutes; you make digital art and need surreal objects or creatures for renders and motion work. Knowing basic 3D concepts helps at the refinement stage, but the site correctly notes none is required to get started.

Skip it when: you need production-ready game assets with clean topology and no cleanup pass; you need engineering-grade CAD with exact dimensions; you want print-and-forget output with zero repair; or you expect a Blender replacement. That last one is worth quoting the team on: their about page says they are not trying to replace professional 3D artists — they are trying to shorten the distance between a thought and something you can hold, print, play with, or sell. Buy the tool for that job, not the other one.

Prompts that work, with examples

The pattern that works mirrors every other generative medium: one clear subject, then finish, then style, then intended use. Vibe words alone (“epic,” “ultra-detailed”) do nothing; concrete nouns do everything.

Desk figurine (text to 3D):

chubby gray cat sitting, closed happy eyes, smooth matte
finish, cute desk figurine style, 3d printable

Product prototype (text to 3D):

matte ceramic table lamp, dome shade, walnut wood base,
soft studio lighting, minimal product-design style,
clean silhouette

Photo reconstruction (image to 3D): upload a clean, well-lit photo with a single subject and an uncluttered background. The walkthrough’s rule holds: cleaner input, better model. Objects that are heavily occluded or photographed from one side only are the hardest case — expect the hidden side to be invented, and inspect it accordingly.

Frequently asked questions

Is Gen3dly free?

New accounts get 20 welcome credits with no card required, and one finished model costs exactly 20 credits — so the free tier buys one complete model with every export format included. After that, the Starter pack is $1.99 one-time for 40 credits (two models), and Plus and Pro run $15 and $30 per month. Treat free as a full trial of the pipeline, not a demo.

Should I use a text prompt or an image as input?

Use an image whenever you have a clean photo of the thing you want: a single subject on an uncluttered background gives the reconstruction the most to work with and locks the identity. Use text when nothing exists yet and you are concepting — a figurine idea, a game prop, a shape you can picture but cannot photograph. Heavily occluded subjects or single-side photos reconstruct poorly either way.

What 3D formats can Gen3dly export?

GLB, GLTF, OBJ, and STL. GLB and GLTF keep materials and suit real-time engines and the web; OBJ and STL are the broadly compatible choices for design tools and slicers. FBX is listed as planned, so confirm it is actually offered in the app before planning a pipeline around it.

Can I sell what I make with Gen3dly?

Commercial usage follows the licence of the plan you are on, plus the terms of the underlying model providers — check the licence shown at export time before an asset goes into a commercial product. For print shops specifically, Gen3dly’s own blog walks through Etsy’s original-design rules for AI-assisted models, which is the compliance layer most sellers skip.

Are Gen3dly models ready for 3D printing?

Not automatically. AI-generated meshes can contain thin walls, non-manifold geometry, or floating parts, so treat every export as a starting point: run a repair pass and a slicing check in your printing software before committing material. Generate-then-finish is the honest workflow — and the finishing toolchain is free.
