OnlineFree.app Review: 105 Free Tools in Your Browser

Published September 16, 2026 · 7 min read

A few weeks ago we reviewed FreeOnline.fyi, a self-built collection of browser tools with unusually good regional calculators — and gave it 4 out of 5, docking a point for a thin catalogue. That critique just aged out: the suite has moved to a new home, OnlineFree.app, and it now counts 105 tools, up from 69. We went back in to see what changed.

Disclosure: FreeToolsHub is built by the same team as FreeOnline.fyi / OnlineFree.app. We label the suite “Editor’s Pick” and recommend third-party tools on merit.

The move: same suite, new address

OnlineFree.app is the new home of the FreeOnline tools suite — same “Useful Tools. A Simpler Internet” tagline, same five-category layout (calculators, converters, generators, formatters, plus a “more tools” shelf), same client-side philosophy. The branding still reads FreeOnline.fyi in places, which tells you the migration is fresh. At the time of writing both addresses serve the site; the new one is the address to bookmark, and as a bonus the .app top-level domain enforces HTTPS by design — a fitting home for a privacy-first toolkit.

What’s new among the 105 tools

Thirty-six new tools is a serious month of building, and the new arrivals lean practical with a streak of playfulness. Highlights from the current catalogue:

  • Favicon and app-icon generator — the fiddly little job every new site dreads, solved.
  • WebM to MP4 converter and an images-to-PDF converter — the two “why is this awkward everywhere else” conversions.
  • CSS gradient generator and a meta description generator — small web-dev helpers that belong in every bookmark bar.
  • D&D name generator and a photo-to-maze generator — the playful shelf expanding on schedule.
  • A free unit converter and word/character counter rounding out the everyday essentials.

The classics from our first review are all still there: the India GST and Alinma mortgage calculators, the Morocco end-of-service calculator, the 401(k) projector, the receipt maker, the background remover — regional depth and all.

What didn’t change (the good parts)

The three things that earned the suite its original recommendation are intact on the new domain: everything runs client-side (nothing uploaded, nothing stored), no accounts anywhere, and the regional, multilingual coverage — EN, FR, DE, AR language tags and locale-specific calculators for the US, UK, India, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Morocco and France — remains the feature no competitor copies. The honest caveats from round one also carry over: there’s still no editorial layer of ratings or “best for” guidance, and discovery leans on the search box.

Does it score higher now?

Yes — by a notch. The catalogue-depth criticism was our main deduction last time, and 105 tools spread across three pages directly answers it. What would take the suite to a full five: ratings or curated collections on top of the raw feed. Until then: 4.5 out of 5, and a standing bookmark.

Frequently asked questions

Has FreeOnline.fyi moved to OnlineFree.app?

Effectively, yes. The FreeOnline tools suite now lives at OnlineFree.app — same branding, same tagline, same client-side tools, now 105 of them. At the time of writing both domains still serve the site, but the .app address is the one to bookmark.

How many tools are there now?

105, up from 69 when we first reviewed the suite. The new arrivals lean practical and playful: a favicon and app-icon generator, WebM to MP4 conversion, an images-to-PDF converter, a CSS gradient generator, a D&D name generator and a meta description generator among them.

Is the new domain still free and signup-free?

Yes. The core philosophy is unchanged — every tool runs client-side in your browser, nothing is uploaded, and there are no accounts. The .app domain also enforces HTTPS by design, which suits a privacy-first toolkit.

Do old freeonline.fyi links still work?

At the time of writing, yes — the original domain still serves the site alongside the new one. Update your bookmarks to onlinefree.app anyway; new domains are exactly the kind of thing that eventually start redirecting.
