Remove Background From a PNG Logo Without the White Halo

Published October 13, 2026 · 8 min read

Logos are the easiest background to remove and the easiest to ruin. Easy because a mark has hard edges and flat colour, the case automatic matting handles best. Ruined because a logo almost always lands on top of something else — a dark footer, a photograph, a video lower-third — and a flat colour meeting a background is exactly where a sloppy matte becomes visible at 12 pixels high.

What is actually in your logo file

Three states, routinely confused:

  • Flattened raster with a white background. The common hand-me-down: someone exported a JPG or flattened a PNG, and the white is real pixels in every row.
  • Already transparent. Check before cutting anything. identify -format "%[channels]" logo.png reporting matte or alpha (ImageMagick) means the transparency is already there; if it looks white, the white is behind it, in the viewer or the page.
  • Vector. SVG, PDF or AI. There is no background to remove — a vector has no canvas fill unless one was drawn — and it renders crisply at any size.

If you have the vector, stop and use it. PNG is a bitmap format: fixed pixel dimensions, and scaling a small logo raster up is resampling, not resolution. The correct order of operations is get the vector, export the sizes you need, and only fall back to cutting a flattened bitmap when that is genuinely all you have.

Cutting the flattened bitmap

  • Segmentation — drop the file into a browser cut-out and take the transparent PNG. Free, no account, no upload, up to 15 MB in. Marks with holes and counters (the ring in an O, the gap in a monogram) come out correctly because the model reasons about the subject, not the colour.
  • Colour key — flat white background plus flat-colour mark: a magic-wand selection at low tolerance is quick and exact, and dangerous in one specific way. Any white inside the mark — a letterform, a knockout detail, a highlight — goes with the background. Select the mark itself and inverse-delete instead.

A mark that is genuinely white on a coloured background is a third case: you cannot key it out, and there is no background to segment. Export a white-on-transparent file from the vector source, or paint the mask by hand in GIMP or Photopea. Twelve minutes with a pen tool beats a day of halo debugging.

Why it glows on dark backgrounds: straight alpha

This is the part that confuses people who are otherwise competent with an editor. PNG stores straight, non-premultiplied alpha: colour and opacity are separate numbers. An edge pixel that is 50% opaque, sampled over a white background, holds a whitish colour with half alpha. Over white, invisible. Over a dark footer, that whitish colour contributes — a pale rim around every letter.

  1. Contract the matte by roughly one pixel at the export size before flattening onto any new background.
  2. Never fix a halo with a stroke or an outer glow: that hides it at one background colour and announces it at every other.
  3. If you must reuse a haloed file, place it on the target background colour first, key that colour out, then verify against black and against white.
  4. If the mark is small and the surfaces vary, export a variant per surface — or ship the vector and let the browser render it.

Putting a background back on purpose

A transparent logo is often only half a deliverable. The other half is versions with a fill, for platforms that will choose the fill for you badly — a dark-mode nav that flattens a transparent mark onto black renders an invisible black mark. The fix is not transparency, it is two files.

  • Cut the background, then use the tool’s custom-colour fill to export a light-on-dark and a dark-on-light variant in the same sitting.
  • Keep the transparent master and generate filled variants from it, rather than re-cutting each time.
  • Test both variants against the real surfaces, including the app-icon mask that crops your artwork to a squircle.
  • If the mark came out small or soft, read what 4x upscaling does and does not recover before you upscale a logo and cut it afterwards.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a logo background transparent for free?

Drop the flattened PNG or JPG into an on-device cut-out and export a transparent PNG — no account and no upload when the tool runs locally. If you hold the vector, export from that instead and skip cutting altogether; you get clean edges at every size.

Why does my transparent logo have a white outline?

Because the edge pixels were sampled against the original white background, and PNG carries that colour alongside the alpha. Preview against black to see it, tighten the matte by about a pixel, and avoid fake fixes such as an outer stroke that only hides the problem on one background.

Can I remove the background from a white logo?

Not by colour — subject and background are the same value. Key the background out only if it is a different colour, and otherwise get a white-on-transparent export from the source file. If the mark sits on a photograph, cut the photograph first and then isolate the mark.

Which format should I deliver a logo in?

SVG where the source is vector, PNG as the universal transparent raster, WebP or AVIF for web pages where bytes matter, never JPEG. Deliver light and dark variants rather than trusting transparency to survive an unknown background.

Is it safe to process a brand mark online?

It is safe in the sense that nothing is uploaded when the model runs in your browser — which is also how the tool can be free and unlimited. A published mark is usually low-stakes; a pre-launch logo is not, and the local route removes the question entirely.
