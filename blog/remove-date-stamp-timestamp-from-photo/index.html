Remove a Date Stamp From a Photo — and Strip the Timestamp Separately

Published October 13, 2026 · 7 min read

“Remove the date from my photo” means one of two completely different things, and picking the wrong tool wastes an afternoon. An orange timestamp baked into the image is pixels and needs inpainting. A date stored in the file’s EXIF block is metadata and never appears on screen at all — it needs a metadata strip. Here is how to tell which you have, and both fixes.

Which problem do you actually have?

  • Visible in the picture — usually orange or white, bottom-right, in the blocky font of a compact camera, phone app or dashcam. This is drawn into the image when the photo is taken. Pixels. Needs inpainting.
  • Invisible in the picture, visible in properties — the capture time stored as DateTimeOriginal in EXIF, or CreateDate in XMP. No editing of the image is involved; the value travels with the file and is displayed by viewers, galleries and upload forms. Metadata. Needs stripping.
  • Both — extremely common in camera-JPEG output, and the reason a scan of an old print can still report a camera date while the orange numerals sit visibly in the corner.

Quick test: view the image full-screen on a plain background. If you can see the date, you have problem one. If you cannot but a file inspector or an upload form shows a capture time, you have problem two.

The visible stamp: inpaint it

A date stamp is a kind thing to remove: it is small, sits in a corner, and the surrounding area is usually one continuous thing — lawn, road, wall, carpet, sky. That is close to the ideal case for a LaMa-class inpainting model (paper, code), which rebuilds masked texture from the wider neighbourhood instead of blurring it.

  1. Open the photo in WatermarkRemover. It takes PNG, JPG or WebP up to 25 MB and needs no account; the first run downloads a model of roughly 200 MB and caches it.
  2. Zoom in. Brush the numerals with a mask that extends a couple of pixels beyond every edge, including any soft glow around the digits — the halo is what gives a removal away.
  3. Run the erase once. Inspect at 100%.
  4. If a ghost remains, paint just the ghost and run again. A second small pass beats one careless mask.
  5. Export PNG, so you do not re-compress the repaired patch.

Nothing is uploaded, which for scanned family snapshots is the point: the pictures you care most about are the ones you least want sent to somebody’s queue.

The invisible one: strip the metadata

For the stored capture time, no image editing is involved. ImageMagick re-writes the file without its profiles:

magick input.jpg -strip output.jpg        # drop EXIF, XMP, ICC and other profiles
magick identify -format "%[EXIF:*]" x.jpg # look before and after to confirm
ffmpeg -i in.jpg -map_metadata -1 -update 1 out.jpg   # same idea via ffmpeg

Stripping is not cosmetic: EXIF also carries GPS coordinates, camera serial numbers and device settings, and any file you post can leak them. Our guide to what inpainting does and does not touch explains why pixel edits leave metadata in place, which is exactly why you want both steps.

Both, in one sitting: inpaint the visible stamp, export PNG, then strip metadata on the way to a JPG for upload. Two minutes, and the file is genuinely clean.

Keeping the result looking like a photograph

  • Work from the highest-resolution copy you have — a re-scan at 600–1200 dpi beats trying to clean a small, already-compressed JPEG.
  • Fix the stamp before any upscaling, not after: repairing 300 pixels is easier than repairing 4,800, and see what upscaling can and cannot recover.
  • Do colour and exposure corrections before inpainting, so the model matches the tone it is filling into.
  • Keep a PNG master and make the share JPEG from it — each JPG save compounds.

If your goal is a general clean-up of old prints rather than one corner, the whole pipeline — scan settings, scratch repair, upscaling, colour — is in the old photo enhancement guide. More free editors and utilities are listed on our image tools page.

Frequently asked questions

How do I remove a date stamp from a photo for free?

Paint over the digits with an inpainting tool and re-run on any leftover. WatermarkRemover does this in the browser with no account and no upload, up to 25 MB per image, with roughly a 200 MB one-time model download. Export PNG so you do not recompress the repaired patch.

Does removing the visible date also remove the EXIF date?

No. Inpainting changes pixels only; the capture time lives in the file’s metadata and stays put. Strip it separately, for example with ImageMagick’s -strip, which also removes GPS and camera serial data.

Why is there still a faint rectangle after I erased the stamp?

Usually the drop shadow or glow around the digits, which is a different colour from the numerals but part of the same mark. Extend the mask a couple of pixels past every edge and run a second, smaller pass.

Can the date be removed if it covers someone’s shoes?

Partially. General inpainting is excellent over lawn, road and wall, and weak where it must invent anatomy. Take the background as far as you can automatically, then finish the subject boundary by hand in a free editor with a clone stamp.

Will a photo editing app leave a trace that it was edited?

It can: re-saving changes compression signatures and strips or rewrites metadata, and a heavily inpainted region has different noise statistics from the rest of the frame. If provenance matters to you, keep the original file untouched and work on copies.
