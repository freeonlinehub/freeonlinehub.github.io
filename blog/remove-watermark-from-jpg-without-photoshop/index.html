Remove a Watermark From a JPG Without Photoshop: Four Routes

Published October 13, 2026 · 8 min read

You do not need a Photoshop subscription to clean a JPG. You need one of four things instead, and the right choice depends on how large the mark is, what it covers, how many images you have, and whether you are willing to send the file to somebody’s server. This is the comparison with the trade-offs stated plainly.

Route 1, in practice

WatermarkRemover is the on-device option, and its published behaviour is what you should judge any tool in this class against: a LaMa-class inpainting model (see the paper) runs on your own CPU or GPU, no image is uploaded, there is no account, no credit system, no watermark and no daily cap — the reason being that a local model has no per-image server cost to bill for. Input is PNG, JPG or WebP up to 25 MB; output is PNG; only the painted region changes, at full resolution; and if your browser lacks WebGPU it falls back to WebAssembly.

The catch is the first run. The model is roughly 200 MB, downloaded once and cached. On a metered connection or an older laptop that is the price of admission, and it is a real cost that a hosted service hides.

The JPG problem, and why the output is PNG

A marked photo is usually already a JPEG, which has already quantized your image into 8×8 blocks — the format is defined in ITU-T T.81. Editing it and saving again re-quantizes everything, generation after generation. The result around a cleaned mark is a soft halo where two compressions disagreed.

Order of operations that avoids it:

  1. Open the original JPG in the tool.
  2. Paint and erase; export PNG, which is lossless.
  3. Do any further retouching on the PNG.
  4. Convert to JPG once, at the end, at a quality around 90–95, only if the destination demands JPG.

If you need to check what you are working with, magick identify -format "%[quality] %[define:jpeg:sample-factor] %[size]-%[dimensions]" x.jpg reports enough to tell a 95-quality original from a thrice-saved one (ImageMagick).

Route 2: the manual route, when it wins

For a 40-pixel mark on an evenly lit wall, a clone stamp in GIMP takes twenty seconds and gives a pixel-perfect result, with no model to download and no browser compatibility question. It wins whenever the surrounding texture is simple and the mark is small, and loses whenever you need to sample from further away than a brush width — which is precisely where a wide-receptive-field inpainting model earns its keep.

Photopea covers the same ground in a browser tab if you would rather not install anything, and it is listed with the rest of the options on our free image tools page.

Route 3: what hosted services actually cost you

Two costs, not one. The obvious cost is money: most “free” watermark removers meter a handful of credits and price the rest, which is the same commercial shape as the hosted background-removal tools in this category — for reference, remove.bg is built on a credit model. The less obvious cost is the upload: your photographs go to a server, get processed there, and sit under somebody’s retention policy. For a holiday album, that is the difference between a tool and a data request. The local route exists exactly for this.

Choose by situation

  • One photo, mark over a wall or sky: clone stamp in a free editor — fastest of all.
  • Several photos, marks over varied backgrounds: on-device inpainting.
  • Family or client photos you do not want uploaded: on-device. Non-negotiable.
  • Hundreds of images with the same corner mark: none of the free routes. A hosted API, or a scripted crop, or renegotiate the source.
  • Mark covering a face: expect partial results from every route. This is a compositional problem, not a tool problem.

Frequently asked questions

How do I remove a watermark from a JPG without Photoshop?

Either paint over it with an inpainting model that runs in your browser, which handles texture rather than smearing it, or use a clone stamp in a free desktop editor for small marks on flat areas. Both avoid a subscription; the browser route is faster on anything complicated.

Why does the cleaned area look soft after I save as JPG?

Because you re-compressed an already-compressed file, and the block boundaries around your repaired patch disagree with the original quantization. Export PNG from the edit, do your other work, and convert to JPG once at the end at high quality.

Is there a truly free watermark remover with no limits?

On-device ones, because there is no server cost per image. WatermarkRemover publishes no account, no credits and no cap, up to 25 MB per image, in exchange for a one-time ~200 MB model download. Hosted services that are free are usually metered, since somebody is paying for the GPU.

Is it safe to upload a watermarked photo to an online remover?

That depends on the photo and the provider’s retention terms, so read them. If the images are family photographs, client proofs or unreleased products, prefer a tool that never uploads — which is the main practical argument for the on-device route besides cost.

Do I need to keep the watermark for provenance?

Sometimes, yes. Watermarks and embedded metadata are how attribution travels with an image, and US law treats removing copyright management information as its own violation. If you are publishing someone else’s work, the mark is a signal you should read rather than erase.
