How to Turn Any Video Into a Prompt — 27 Platforms and Local Files

Published October 13, 2026 · 7 min read

“Any video” is a claim worth testing, because the interesting part is not the headline platforms — it is the awkward ones. Here is what the supported surface actually looks like, when uploading a file beats pasting a link, and the five source types that reliably produce bad prompts no matter which tool you use.

What “any” really covers

SocialToPrompt names 27 platforms on its homepage: YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, X, Vimeo, Twitch, Reddit, Bilibili, Kuaishou, Xiaohongshu, Pinterest, Snapchat, LinkedIn, Dailymotion, Rumble, Kick, SoundCloud, Mixcloud, Bandcamp, Twitter, Tumblr, 9GAG, VK, Niconico, PeerTube and Odysee. More usefully, it says anything yt-dlp supports will work — a community-maintained downloader that tracks several hundred sites — plus direct upload of MP4, MOV and WebM.

Between the named list and the yt-dlp umbrella, the honest statement is: if it is a public video at a URL, it probably works; if it is not public, no tool can read it. The exceptions are the interesting part, and they are below.

The long tail of platforms

Different sources need different handling, and knowing the category saves a wasted credit:

  • Vertical short-form (TikTok, Reels, Shorts, Kuaishou, Xiaohongshu): fast cuts and on-screen text. See TikTok and Instagram Reels.
  • Long-form (YouTube, Vimeo, Twitch VOD): always excerpt to the shot you care about rather than submitting the whole video.
  • Aggregators (Reddit, 9GAG, Tumblr, Pinterest): the link may point at an embedded host rather than a file — our Reddit and Pinterest guides cover resolving those.
  • Audio-first (SoundCloud, Mixcloud, Bandcamp): these are in the platform list, but a static artwork image gives the visual pipeline nothing beyond “cover art”. Not a prompt source.
  • Regional and niche hosts (VK, Niconico, PeerTube, Odysee, Rumble, Dailymotion, Kick): usually fine, occasionally rate-limited by the host rather than the tool.

Platform-specific mechanics live in the YouTube, Facebook and X/Twitter guides.

Upload instead of paste

Three cases where the file route is simply better. The clip was never published — a screen recording, a phone take, a client export. The post is deleted or geo-restricted, and the only copy you have is the one on your drive. Or you want to skip the extraction step: the site bills extraction at 1 credit and prompt generation at 3, so a file you already hold spends your budget only on the reading.

Practical form: keep a clips/ folder with 10–20 second excerpts cut to the shot you are studying, named by mechanism (elevator-dolly-in.mp4), which is also how you avoid re-downloading the same reference from three platforms. If the file came from someone else, the rights question in the reverse-engineering method still applies.

Sources that break extraction

  1. Login-gated, private or age-restricted posts. Nothing anonymous can reach them; no tool fixes this.
  2. Stories and disappearing content. The URL is gone by the time you paste it, and hosts actively block automated reads.
  3. Drone-shot embeds with an API behind them — some Pinterest and Facebook watch URLs resolve to a wrapper page rather than media.
  4. Region-blocked or censored videos. The host refuses the request; the tool reports it as unsupported.
  5. Playlists and channel URLs. Submit a single video URL.

Workaround for four of the five: download the clip yourself (where you are entitled to) and upload the file — see downloading social videos and converting an MP4 to a prompt.

Choosing a source that extracts well

Extraction quality is a property of the clip as much as the model. The best sources share a profile: one continuous shot rather than three, visible subject separation, real camera movement (not just fast cutting), legible lighting, and no wall of burned-in captions. The worst are screen recordings, talking heads on tripod-locked cameras, music videos whose style is the content, and clips under two seconds.

Pick sources on that profile deliberately when you are building a library, and you will spend far fewer credits on repair work. What a finished prompt should contain, once the source cooperates, is in the field-by-field guide; the waiting-room arithmetic of the free tier is in what 3 credits buy.

Frequently asked questions

Can I turn any video into an AI prompt?

Nearly any public video at a resolvable URL, plus files you already own. SocialToPrompt lists 27 platforms and supports anything yt-dlp can reach, with MP4, MOV and WebM upload. What it cannot reach is anything login-gated, private, age-restricted or disappearing.

Should I paste a link or upload a file?

Paste when the clip is public and you want speed. Upload when the clip is not published, when the link will not resolve, or when you want to skip the extraction charge — extraction and prompt generation are billed separately, so owning the file means spending credits only on the analysis.

Do long videos work?

Poorly, and it is a quality problem rather than a technical one. A single prompt cannot carry eight minutes of shot changes, and the stated credit window covers clips up to 20 seconds. Excerpt the shot you actually want to recreate.

Which sources produce the best extracted prompts?

Single continuous shots with real camera movement, visible subject separation and legible lighting. Screen recordings, locked-off talking heads, heavily captioned clips and sub-two-second excerpts produce the worst.
