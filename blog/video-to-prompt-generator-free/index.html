Video to Prompt Generator Free: What the No-Cost Tier Actually Buys

Published October 13, 2026 · 8 min read

“Free video to prompt generator” is a phrase that means four different things depending on which page you land on: genuinely free and unlimited, free until you hit a wall, free only if you bring your own model, or free as in a 7-day trial with a card attached. SocialToPrompt is the second kind, and it publishes its arithmetic on the homepage. Once you do the sum yourself, the decision of whether to touch it gets much easier — so let’s start with the numbers rather than the adjectives.

The credit maths, stated plainly

Everything the tool charges for is on its own FAQ, so there is no guesswork:

  • New accounts get 3 credits, no credit card required.
  • Those free credits expire 24 hours after registration — not 30 days, not never.
  • Extracting a video costs 1 credit; generating the prompt costs 3. The site states a full video-to-prompt run is 4 credits for videos up to 20 seconds.
  • 3 < 4. That is the whole catch: the free allowance does not cover a complete extract-and-prompt cycle, because you need one credit more than you are given.

This is not a hidden fee or a bait-and-switch — the numbers are printed openly, and one-time packs start at $2.90 with no subscription. But it does mean the honest answer to “is there a free video to prompt generator?” is: free to test the halves, not free to complete a run. Anyone comparing tools on price should compare them in credits-per-outcome rather than headline words, which is exactly what our comparison of free options does across the whole field.

If your plan is “get the clip and study it myself”, you can do three of those for nothing. If it is “get me a prompt I can paste into Veo”, budget for a pack.

Your first run, step by step

  1. Sign in first, decide later. Registration is where the 3 credits appear, and the 24-hour clock starts then — so do not sign up on a Friday night if you plan to work over the weekend.
  2. Paste a single video link (or upload an MP4, MOV or WebM). The homepage lists 27 named platforms — YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, X, Reddit, Pinterest, Bilibili, Kuaishou, Xiaohongshu and others — and says it supports anything yt-dlp can reach, which is a practical way of saying “most of the web”.
  3. Pick your output language. The prompt can come back in around 20 languages; if you write prompts in a language other than English, this quietly saves a translation pass.
  4. Press Download Video or Extract prompt. Two separate buttons, two separate charges — which is how you can spend the trial on just one half.
  5. Read the staged analysis. The interface narrates what it is doing: extracting frames, reading video content, understanding motion, analysing camera and movement, analysing style, detecting visual aesthetics, generating the prompt. The site says this typically takes 20–40 seconds. Watch the motion and camera lines especially: those are the stages whose output you’ll want to verify against the clip.
  6. Copy or download the .txt. Keep it — a prompt library is the compounding asset here, not the one clip you generated.

The mechanics of what the returned prompt contains are worth reading separately: our walkthrough of converting a video into an AI prompt breaks down the fields, and the reverse-engineering method covers what to do with them afterwards.

How to spend a trial you only get once

Because the credits expire in a day and you cannot accumulate them, spend the trial on a controlled comparison instead of the first video that annoyed you:

  • Pick three clips that differ structurally: one single-shot piece (a product on a turntable), one with real camera movement (a walking POV or a drone shot), and one fast-cut multi-shot edit. The gaps in any extractor show up on the third.
  • Score the output on six things: subject, camera move, lighting, pacing, style/grade, and set. If the tool names the camera move correctly, it has done the job most descriptions skip — that vocabulary is the whole reason a prompt like this beats “a person walks down a street”.
  • Test the length boundary deliberately. The stated 4-credit run covers videos up to 20 seconds; feed it 45 and see what it says about the cost before you commit a credit. Knowing the long-clip economics on day one prevents a nasty surprise at clip twelve.
  • Generate once in your own output language, so you know whether you ever need that translation step again.

When $2.90 stops being annoying

The packs are one-time purchases with a 30-day validity: 50 credits for $2.90, 150 for $5.90, 400 for $12.90. At 4 credits per full run the entry pack is about a dozen prompts, and about 50 downloads if that is all you use it for. Two observations worth making before you buy:

  • Per-attempt cost is only meaningful if the output survives contact with a generator. A $0.24 prompt you paste once and use is cheap; a $0.24 prompt that needs four re-runs because the description was vague is not.
  • The 30-day expiry punishes hoarding. Buying 400 credits for an idea you test once a month is a waste; buy the small pack, use it, and repurchase if the workflow stuck.

If the cost model is the thing that decides it for you, read the no-cost-forever alternatives before paying anything.

The truly free route, and its real cost

You can build the same output without ever creating an account, and it costs time instead of credits: download the clip with yt-dlp, sample frames with ffmpeg, paste the frames into any vision model and ask for a structured description with shot, camera, lighting, pacing and style fields. What you get is free and private, and what you pay is the ten minutes per clip plus the fact that a frame sampler cannot see motion — so the camera-movement half, which is the valuable half, is guesswork. The one thing neither route gives you is rights: a prompt describes technique rather than content, but generating a recreation of someone’s ad, character or likeness is a different act from studying its shot list — see the fair-use guidance before you ship anything close.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free video to prompt generator?

Yes, with an account. SocialToPrompt gives every new account 3 credits without a credit card, and the site states those free credits are valid for 24 hours after registration. A complete video-to-prompt run costs 4 credits — 1 to extract the video and 3 to generate the prompt — so three free credits do not quite cover one full run.

What can I do with 3 free credits?

Three downloads on their own, or one prompt generation without a separate extraction, or one download plus two credits left over. If you only want the clip file, the free allowance covers three platform links before you pay anything.

How much does it cost after the free credits?

The site sells one-time credit packs rather than a subscription: 50 credits for $2.90, 150 for $5.90 and 400 for $12.90, each valid 30 days from purchase. At 4 credits per full run, the cheapest pack works out to roughly a dozen prompts.

Do I need an account to use it?

Yes. The tool is credit-based, so it requires sign-in before analysis. If you want a zero-account workflow, the DIY route — pull frames with a local command-line tool and describe them to a vision model yourself — needs no account at all, at the cost of far more manual work.
